micro:bit V2 · DFRobot micro:Driver

dfrobot‑rover

A rover that drives on two servo wheels and looks around with an ultrasonic sensor on a third. You drive it from this browser — nothing to install.

▶ Launch the remote opens the control app · press Connect

Needs Chrome or Edge — Safari and Firefox have no Web Bluetooth. Turn the rover on first, then press Connect: its controls appear by themselves, because the robot sends its own panel.

Where everything plugs in

Match the colour of the wire to the colour of the port — no pin numbers to read.

Get it running

  1. Wire it up as above, batteries last, switch off.
  2. Open makecode.microbit.org → Extensions, and paste both URLs, one at a time: https://github.com/DFRobot/pxt-motor for the wheels and https://github.com/tinkertanker/pxt-oled-ssd1306 for the screen. Paste the URLs — don't search, the first is named just motor and has many look-alikes.
  3. Open the gear icon → Project Settings and turn on No Pairing Required and the Bluetooth UART service; leave the other Bluetooth services off. Without the first one, nothing will connect.
  4. Switch to the JavaScript view, paste rover-remote.ts, download to the micro:bit.
  5. Switch the rover on, press Launch the remote above, then Connect.

Quick facts

Boardmicro:bit V2 — V1 hasn't enough memory for Bluetooth plus the strip and screen
WheelsTwo 360° servos on S1 and S2, driven over I²C — they use no pins
Sensor headA third servo on S3 — aim it by hand, or set Head to Sweep and it pans the room on its own
Screen128×32 OLED on the I²C port, address 0x3C
DistanceHC-SR04P — trig P13, echo P14, powered at 3.3 V
Lights8 NeoPixels on P15
Power4×AA to the DC socket, 3.5–5.5 V — never run the servos from USB
Free pinsP0 P1 P2 P8 P12 P16

Before the first power-on: measure the V pin on the P13 or P14 socket. It should read about 3.3 V — the distance sensor's echo copies whatever voltage you feed it, and 5 V would go straight onto a 3.3 V pin.
